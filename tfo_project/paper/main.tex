\title{Reading a magnet's Hamiltonian term by term:\\
two-dimensional terahertz spectroscopy as vertex spectroscopy of
TmFeO$_3$}

\author{Working draft}
\affiliation{---}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
One-dimensional spectroscopy measures the \emph{spectrum} of a
Hamiltonian; two-dimensional coherent spectroscopy (2DCS) measures its
\emph{vertices}---the individual nonlinear terms that convert one
coherence into another.  We show that in the rare-earth orthoferrite
TmFeO$_3$ this correspondence can be made one-to-one and, for every
observed feature, explicit.  Time-reversal parity and space-group irrep
bookkeeping reduce all symmetry-allowed Fe--Tm couplings to exactly two
conversion vertices; the mirror at the Tm $4c$ site then assigns each
light polarisation to one block of the crystal-field qutrit, fixing what
each channel can excite and what it can radiate.  Driven by the measured
single-cycle waveform, with every damping rate set by a measured
linewidth and every emission weight by a measured oscillator strength,
one Hamiltonian reproduces both cross-polarised inventories without
retuning and four of six same-polarised amplitudes within $15\%$.  We
give the pathway of every peak in all four channels---which vertex
permits it, which coherence occupies the delay axis, which multipole
radiates, and what would falsify the assignment---and show that the
residual discrepancy reduces to a single dynamical ratio.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Linear response cannot distinguish two Hamiltonians with the same normal
modes: absorption measures pole positions and dipole weights, and any
coupling that merely hybridises modes is absorbed into the mode basis.
What it can never see is a \emph{conversion}: a coherence prepared on one
transition and re-emitted on another.  That is precisely what a cross
peak in 2DCS certifies~\cite{Lu2017,Mukamel}, and a conversion requires a
specific anharmonic vertex.  A cross peak at $(\omega_\tau,\omega_t)$ is
therefore not a feature to be fitted but a \emph{measurement of one
coupling term}: the delay axis names the stored coherence, the detection
axis the emitter, and the signed side constrains the pathway order.  If
symmetry makes the space of candidate vertices finite---and in a crystal
it does---a polarisation-resolved 2D atlas can be read as the Hamiltonian
itself.

TmFeO$_3$ is an unusually clean testbed because two qualitatively
different subsystems share one THz octave.  Below the spin reorientation
the Fe sublattice orders in $\Gamma_2$
$(F_x,C_y,G_z)$~\cite{Yamaguchi,Kimel2004,Zhang2016}, with
quasi-ferromagnetic and quasi-antiferromagnetic magnons at $\qfm=0.38$
and $\qafm=0.90$\,THz, while the three lowest Tm$^{3+}$ crystal-field
(CEF) singlets form a qutrit with $E_{12}=0.50$, $E_{23}=0.70$,
$E_{13}=1.20$\,THz.  A single-cycle pulse covers all five modes, and
measurements in the cross- and same-polarised channels of two field
geometries return distinct, reproducible inventories.

\section{Model, and where it is exact}

The Fe$^{3+}$ ($S=5/2$) sublattice is classical, with the calibrated
orthoferrite Hamiltonian (exchange, single-ion anisotropy,
Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya) reproducing $\qfm$, $\qafm$ and $\Gamma_2$.  Each
Tm$^{3+}$ is truncated to its three lowest singlets and carried as the
Gell-Mann vector $\lambda^a=\langle\Lambda^a\rangle$.  Because every
single-ion term is linear in the $\Lambda^a$, the classical
$\vec\lambda$ equation of motion \emph{is} the exact von Neumann
equation of the driven, damped qutrit, and a Boltzmann ensemble over
level seeds is the exact thermal density matrix [SM, Sec.~S1].  The
Fe--Tm sector is built by projecting $\ve S^{T}\!A\,\ve J$ into the
qutrit in a transported gauge over the four Tm sublattices [SM,
Secs.~S2--S3].  All spectra use the measured pump (spectrum peaked at
$0.53$\,THz, no DC), measured linewidths as damping rates, and measured
oscillator strengths as emission weights.  Peaks are identified blindly
as 2D local maxima with prominence $\geq1.5$; the $\tau$-averaged signal
is subtracted at each detection time, removing the $\omega_\tau=0$ row.

\section{Two vertices survive}

A conversion vertex must (R1) reach the bright coordinate, (R2) be
sourced by the pumped mode, (R3) be time-reversal even, (R4) be
$\Gamma_1$-allowed in the ordered state, (R5) carry the frequency
mismatch, and (R6) engage both pulses to survive
$\Mnl=M_{AB}-M_A-M_B$.  These exhaust the coupling space [SM, Sec.~S4].
The static bilinear $K^-S\lambda$ fails threefold---linear in $S$ it
hybridises but cannot convert, its channels are $\Gamma_1$-cancelled to
machine precision, and its one allowed component reorients the ground
state---and the quadratic families $W_4$, $W_1^{xy}$, $W_6$,
$\kappa^E$ are exactly inert.  What remains is
\begin{align}
  H_W&=W^{yy}_1\,S_y^2\,\lambda^1, &
  H_{\kappa}&=\kappa^B_{1y}\,B_y(t)\,S_y\,\lambda^1 ,
  \label{eq:vertices}
\end{align}
together with the static hybridisation $W^{xz}_1S_xS_z\lambda^1$ and the
population channel $W_3S_\alpha S_\beta\lambda^3$.  Every peak below is
built from these.

\section{Symmetry assigns each polarisation one block}
\label{sec:blocks}

The Tm $4c$ site carries the mirror $m$ ($z\to-z$), under which
$d_{x,y}$ are even and $d_z$ odd, while $m_z$ is even and $m_{x,y}$ odd.
The projected moment matrix---an output of the $PJP$ construction, not an
input---has $\lambda^2$ carrying only $\mu_z$ and $\lambda^{5,7}$ only
$\mu_{x,y}$, which forces $\ket1,\ket2$ to share a parity and $\ket3$ to
be opposite.  Every coupling follows:

Reciprocity---one operator per polarisation, driving and radiating
alike---then implies that \emph{cross-polarised detection reads exactly
the block the pulse did not drive}.  This is the structural reason
cross-polarisation isolates conversion, and it carries two independent
checks: it predicts the geometry-B subalgebra closure, where the
$c$-directed drive touches only $\mu^z_{12}$ so that
$\lambda^{4\text{--}7}$ vanish identically (verified: exactly $0$,
against $\lambda^{1,2,3}\sim2.6\times10^{-2}$); and in geometry A
$F_z\equiv0$ to $10^{-15}$, so the Fe sublattice is radiatively silent
in that cross channel and anything seen there at a magnon frequency must
be Tm.

Two effects extend this.  Below $T_N$ the $\Gamma_2$ order parameter is
$m_z$-odd and breaks the site mirror, inducing a $c$-axis coupling
$d_z^{\rm eff}$ to $1\!\leftrightarrow\!2$ that parity would forbid; this
is what lets $E\!\parallel\!c$ create the $E_{12}$ coherence dominating
the geometry-A delay axis.  And the $4c$ site has no inversion (Fe at
$4b$ does), so the emitted moment may carry a quadratic term,
\begin{equation}
  m_z=\mu\,\lambda^2+\beta\,\lambda^1\lambda^2 ,
  \label{eq:hyper}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal T$-odd as a magnetisation must be, radiating at $2\omega_{12}$.

\section{The pathways, peak by peak}

We now give every feature of Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas}: what makes it
possible, how the excitation reaches the emitter, and what would falsify
the assignment.  Throughout, the delay axis carries a coherence that
\emph{never radiates}---it need only be excitable and able to store
phase---while the detection axis is constrained by the emitting
multipole.  That asymmetry does most of the work.

\subsection{Geometry A, cross-polarised}

\emph{$(\qafm,E_{12})=1.00$: the magnetoelectric peak; measures
$W^{yy}_1$.}  The $H\!\parallel\!a$ Zeeman field launches the $\qafm$
magnon, which precesses at $0.90$\,THz through the delay.  It cannot
radiate here---it has no $c$-axis moment---but it need not: it modulates
the Tm crystal field through $W^{yy}_1S_y^2\lambda^1$.  Each pulse
supplies one magnon leg, so the $\Mnl$ subtraction keeps the cross term,
which rectifies,
\begin{equation}
  h^1_{\rm NL}=W^{yy}_1A^2\big[\cos\qafm\tau-\cos\qafm(2t+\tau)\big]
  \theta(t),
\end{equation}
the difference piece being a step switched on at the probe that carries
the delay phase but no $t$ dependence.  Fed into the $E_{12}$ oscillator
it gives $\Mnl\propto\cos(\qafm\tau)[1-\cos E_{12}t]$: a factorised,
rank-one peak.  \emph{Why it is possible at all}: an E1-dark magnon is
made visible by borrowing the Tm dipole.  \emph{Where the energy comes
from}: $\delta S_y^2$ contains only DC and $2\qafm$, so between free
modes this vertex could never emit at $\omega_{12}$; the quantum is drawn
from the pulse edge, an intra-pulse difference-frequency process.  This
is verified rather than asserted---stretching the pulse from
$\sigma=0.25$ to $1.0$ at fixed amplitude collapses the peak by $4.5$
decades, while switching off the direct Tm drive changes it by
$<10^{-4}$.  \emph{Signature}: $\propto A_{\rm Fe}^2$, temperature-flat
below $\sim10$\,K, CEF linewidth along $\omega_t$.

\emph{$(E_{12},2E_{12})=0.97$: second-harmonic emission; measures
$\beta$.}  The pump stores $E_{12}$, setting the delay axis at $0.50$;
the detection axis is its second harmonic at $2E_{12}=1.0000$\,THz.  A
moment linear in the qutrit coordinates cannot radiate a harmonic, which
is why the model missed this peak until Eq.~(\ref{eq:hyper}) was
included; the $4c$ site's lack of inversion permits it, and Fe at $4b$
is excluded from the mechanism.  Evaluated on the trajectories the
quadratic channel is remarkably clean, contributing essentially one peak,
$5.8\times$ its own $(E_{12},E_{12})$ diagonal, identical in local and
global sublattice frames.  \emph{Discriminators}: the line sits at
exactly $2E_{12}$ and \emph{tracks} $E_{12}$, so it does not soften at
the spin reorientation as any magnon-derived feature would; it is one
field order above the main peak; its width is twice the $E_{12}$
linewidth; and it is independent of $W^{xz}_1$ and of the Zeeman drive.

\emph{$(\qafm,\qafm)=0.36$: the magnon diagonal, seen through the Tm
ion.}  Since $F_z\equiv0$ the magnon cannot radiate here at all.  What
radiates is the Tm dipole, which carries a component at the magnon
frequency: in $\Gamma_2$ the static vertex $W^{xz}_1S_xS_z\lambda^1$
contains $G_z\,\delta S_x\,\lambda^1$, a term \emph{linear} in the magnon
fluctuation, which shakes the $E_{12}$ oscillator at $0.90$\,THz.  A
driven oscillator responds at the driving frequency, not its own.  This
is verified in detail: the sideband sits at $0.8999$\,THz---the magnon,
not the $\qfm+E_{12}=0.876$ combination; its amplitude is \emph{linear}
in the Zeeman drive whereas a harmonic would be quadratic; the transfer
coefficient $|\lambda^2|_{0.90}/|m_x|_{0.90}=0.142$--$0.155$ is
drive-independent over a $20\times$ range, the hallmark of a linear
susceptibility evaluated off resonance; and the admixture grows linearly
with $W^{xz}_1$.

\emph{$(\qafm,2\qafm)=0.39$: prediction.}  The same quadratic term must
double \emph{any} frequency present in the Tm dipole, so the
magnon-forced component radiates its harmonic at $1.79$\,THz.  Observing
it confirms Eq.~(\ref{eq:hyper}) independently of the $2E_{12}$ peak; its
absence with $(E_{12},2E_{12})$ present would falsify that equation.

\emph{$(-\qafm,E_{12})=0.52$ and $(\pm E_{12},E_{12})=0.33$.}  The first
is not a separate mechanism: the $W$ vertex is symmetric under the two
time orderings of the magnon coherence, so the signal appears on both
signed sides with the non-rephasing branch stronger.  The second is the
one-quantum diagonal, the directly driven $E_{12}$ read through the same
dipole; its size relative to the cross peaks is a sensitive fluence
diagnostic, falling from $1.45$ at $A_{\rm Fe}=1.2$ to $0.16$ at $0.12$,
which is how the operating point was fixed.

\subsection{Geometry B, cross-polarised}

Here the analysed moment is $m_x$, which the $\qafm$ magnon carries
directly, so both peaks emit at $0.90$\,THz through the Fe magnetic
dipole and the Tm ion is radiatively absent.

\emph{$(\qfm,\qafm)=1.00$: magnon--magnon; measures the Fe sector
alone.}  $H\!\parallel\!c$ launches the quasi-ferromagnetic magnon,
which precesses at $0.38$ through the delay; single-ion anisotropy and
the Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya interaction couple the branches, so the stored
$\qfm$ phase is re-emitted on $\qafm$.  No Fe--Tm coupling enters at any
vertex, which is why this peak is temperature-flat: a pure two-magnon
process, independent of the qutrit populations.

\emph{$(E_{12},\qafm)=0.45$: the gated transfer; measures
$\kappa^B_{1y}$.}  $E\!\parallel\!a$ stores a Tm $E_{12}$ coherence---%
parity-allowed here, since $1\!\leftrightarrow\!2$ is owned by this
polarisation---and a Fe--Tm vertex transfers it to the magnon.  Isolating
the vertices settles which: with $\kappa^B_{1y}$ alone the peak is
present, with the static hybridisation alone it is absent, and \emph{no}
strength of the static vertex recovers it.  A sweep over
$W^{xz}_1\in\{0.01,\dots,0.09\}$ shows the static vertex never creates a
second resolved peak; instead the two delay rows coalesce and the leading
peak migrates ($0.38\to0.40\to0.48$\,THz) while hybridisation-shifted
$E_{12}$ emission leaks in at $0.44$--$0.45$\,THz.  This is the
structural signature of a mixer: a static vertex \emph{moves} peaks
(level repulsion, which also shifts modes off their 1D-measured
positions), whereas a gated converter \emph{adds} a peak at unshifted
lines.  The data show two resolved peaks at the unshifted energies, so
the gate is required.  Physically $\kappa^B$ supplies the
$0.50\to0.90$\,THz mismatch impulsively from the photon while
$B_z(t)\neq0$---and the same gate makes it \emph{identically silent} in
geometry A, where its background would otherwise contaminate the clean
$W^{yy}_1$ peak.  \emph{Signature}: falls as the population difference
$n_1-n_2$ ($0.46\to0.45\to0.40\to0.36$ at $0/5/8/10$\,K), the sharpest
thermal discriminator in the atlas against the temperature-flat
magnon--magnon peak.

\emph{$(-\qfm,\qafm)=0.17$, $(\qafm,\qafm)=0.14$,
$(E_{12}{+}\qafm,\qafm)=0.07$.}  The rephasing partner; the $\qafm$
diagonal, which here is genuine Fe M1 emission unlike its geometry-A
counterpart; and a combination tone, the sum-frequency of the two stored
coherences radiating on the magnon line, which is $W$-fed and so offers
an independent handle on the $W$ scale.

\emph{The post-drive buildup.}  The measured geometry-B signal continues
to grow after the pulse.  No coherent vertex can do this once the
measured linewidths are enforced: because $B\!\parallel\!c$ never drives
$\qafm$ directly, all geometry-B magnon amplitude is \emph{converted},
and a converted signal inherits its source's lifetime---with
$T_2(E_{12})=8$\,ps everything is over within $\sim10$\,ps.  Even the
$\tau$-labelled population grating fails, because its delay label is
written by the $E_{12}$ coherence and is therefore $T_2$-confined.  The
buildup requires the one reservoir outliving every $T_2$: the CEF
populations, fed incoherently by the dissipated pulse energy and acting
on the magnon through $W_3S_\alpha S_\beta\lambda^3$---the mechanism of
thermally driven spin reorientation.  Implemented in the solver, it
reproduces the observation: the $\omega_\tau=E_{12}$ row falls off the
impulsive peak, recovers on the repopulation time, and persists beyond
$100$\,ps, $50$--$100\times$ above the coherent-only model.

\subsection{Geometry A, same-polarised}

The analysed moment is $m_x$, and three contributions reach it, each
required by symmetry and each carrying measured features: the Tm CEF
moments (emitting at $E_{13}$, $E_{23}$), the Fe $F_x$ (magnon
frequencies), and $d_z^{\rm eff}\lambda^2$ (emitting at $E_{12}$), the
last demanded by reciprocity once $d_z^{\rm eff}$ appears in the drive.
Fitting those three weights to the digitised measurement, with the cross
channels held fixed, gives Table~\ref{tab:samepol}.

\emph{The $z$-dark $1\to3$ dipole---measured, not assumed.}  The
measured map contains delay frequencies $\omega_\tau\in\{E_{12},\qafm\}$
only.  A bright $1\!\to\!3$ dipole would store an $E_{13}$ coherence at
first order and produce an $(E_{13},E_{23})$ peak with \emph{exactly}
the same dipole product $\mu_{12}\mu_{13}\mu_{23}$ as the observed
$(E_{12},E_{23})$, so no parameter choice could separate them: the
absence of the whole row is a selection rule.  It does not contradict
the measured $E_{13}$ oscillator strength ($f=8.7$), which lives in the
orthogonal polarisations---the same transition is bright in one
polarisation and dark in another, a fact the 2D map resolves and 1D
absorption, summed over its bright polarisations, does not.  In the fit
the residual admixture is not imposed; it comes out at zero.

\emph{$(E_{12},E_{23})=1.00$: the dominant transfer, via a population.}
With the single-action route closed by the dark dipole, the surviving
pathway acts twice: one probe interaction converts the stored $E_{12}$
coherence into a level-2 \emph{population} that still carries the
$\omega_\tau=E_{12}$ phase, and a second converts that population into
the emitting $E_{23}$ coherence.  Being co-rotating it lands
non-rephasing, the observed side, and it involves $\mu_{13}$ at no
vertex---which is why it survives while its $E_{13}$ partner is
throttled.

\emph{$(E_{12},0)=1.00$: the rectified line.}  Taken at its
zero-frequency output the same double action rectifies the stored
coherence into a slow net magnetisation of the detection window.  This
is the strongest measured feature; in the 2D maps it is suppressed by
construction, since the $t\geq3$ window with Gaussian apodisation removes
$\omega_t\to0$, so it is analysed separately through
$S_{\rm DC}(\tau)=\langle\Mnl\rangle_t$, where it appears as a sharp
line at $+0.49$\,THz.  Its absence from Table~\ref{tab:samepol} is an
analysis-window statement, not a physical one.

\emph{$(E_{12},\qfm)=0.45$ and $(E_{12},\qafm)=0.40$: Tm$\to$magnon,
read through Fe.}  The stored $E_{12}$ coherence is handed to the Fe
sublattice by the static couplings and radiates through $F_x$, which
carries both branches.  These are the reverse of the geometry-A cross
peak---there the magnon feeds the Tm dipole, here the Tm coherence feeds
the magnon---so the static $W$ family is read in both directions.  They
require the Fe term in the detection operator; without it this channel
has no magnon-frequency emission at all.

\emph{$(\qafm,E_{12})=0.30$: the reciprocity partner, observed.}  The
magnon occupies the delay axis and the Tm ion radiates at $E_{12}$ into
the \emph{same}-polarised channel.  That emission is possible only
through $d_z^{\rm eff}$---and reciprocity demands it, because the same
$d_z^{\rm eff}$ is what lets $E\!\parallel\!c$ create the $E_{12}$
coherence in the first place.  We had earlier reported this partner as
absent and treated the mismatch as an open tension; the digitised map
shows it present at $0.30$ of maximum, and the fitted weight reproduces
it at $0.32$.  This peak is the direct measurement of $d_z^{\rm eff}$.

\emph{$(\qafm,E_{13})=0.30$ and $(E_{12},E_{13})=0.15$.}  The first has
the magnon on the delay axis, handed to the Tm ion and emitted at
$E_{13}$; the second is a single probe action rotating the bra of the
stored $\rho_{12}$ into $\rho_{13}$, again co-rotating, with its emission
throttled by the nearly dark $\mu^z_{13}$.  These are the two the model
gets wrong, and Sec.~\ref{sec:open} shows they fail together.

\emph{Two model features not in the measured list, both predictions.}
The \emph{rephasing remnant} $(-E_{12},E_{23})$ at $0.67$: checked
against a standalone exact-qutrit integrator, where the rephasing partner
retains $0.47$--$0.58$ of the dominant peak across all drive strengths,
dipole phases and dampings---an exact floor for any three-level ion under
this pulse.  If the measured map truly contains nothing there, the
suppression must come from outside the point-ion model: quadrant folding
in the display convention, or propagation in a thick sample.  And the
\emph{two-quantum row} $(E_{13},E_{23})$ at $0.32$: an $E_{13}$ delay
coherence does survive the dark dipole, but only at second order, the
pump driving $1\!\to\!2$ and $2\!\to\!3$ in succession rather than
$1\!\to\!3$ directly.  Its weakness relative to the first-order row a
bright $\mu^z_{13}$ would give is exactly why the model is consistent
with an inventory listing no $E_{13}$ delay features.

\subsection{Geometry B, same-polarised: a prediction and a $\beta$ meter}

The detected moment is $m_z$.  The CEF coordinates
$\lambda^{4\text{--}7}$ vanish identically here, so no $E_{13}$ or
$E_{23}$ emission is possible; but $\lambda^2$ is driven \emph{directly}
through $\mu^z_{12}=5.264$, and the resulting Tm signal is $\sim50\times$
the Fe contribution.  The channel is therefore Tm-dominated, and what it
shows depends on $\beta$: with the linear moment alone it would be led by
$(\qfm,\qafm)$ with $E_{12}$ diagonals beneath, whereas with the
$\beta\simeq7\mu$ inferred in geometry A the second harmonic is
$4.1\times$ the magnon--magnon feature and the map collapses to a
\emph{single} peak at $(E_{12},2E_{12})$.  Measuring this channel reads
$\beta$ off directly; either outcome is decisive, which makes it the most
informative measurement still missing.

\section{The one open item}
\label{sec:open}

The two failures in Table~\ref{tab:samepol} are not independent.  Both
peaks are detected at $E_{13}$ and therefore share an emission weight, so
their ratio is a property of the conversion dynamics alone:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{(E_{12},E_{13})}{(\qafm,E_{13})}:\qquad
  \text{model }4.9,\qquad\text{experiment }0.5 .
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
No choice of detection weights can move it, and no rescaling of the drive
does either, since both routes are first order in the coherence they
start from.  The model routes too much $E_{13}$ emission through the
$E_{12}$-excitation row relative to the magnon row---equivalently, the
magnon$\to$Tm handoff feeding $(\qafm,E_{13})$ is too weak relative to
the intra-Tm $\rho_{12}\to\rho_{13}$ rotation.  The natural places to
look are the strength of that handoff at the $E_{13}$ block, which our
vertex set feeds only through $\lambda^1$, and a $\lambda^4$-targeting
vertex, allowed in the $W$ family but so far held at zero.

\section{Falsifiable content}

Fifteen independent observations constrain five couplings
($W^{yy}_1$, $W^{xz}_1$, $\kappa^B_{1y}$, $W_3$, $\beta$), one selection
rule and two drive parameters.  The sharpest tests: any peak emitting at
$\qafm$ inherits the $15\times$ sharper magnon linewidth;
$(\qfm,\qafm)$ is temperature-flat while $(E_{12},\qafm)$ falls as
$n_1-n_2$; rotating either polarisation away from $c$ must revive the
$\omega_\tau=E_{13}$ rows; the $2E_{12}$ line tracks $E_{12}$ and so does
\emph{not} soften at the spin reorientation, whereas every feature
relying on $d_z^{\rm eff}\propto\langle F_x\rangle$---the
$E_{12}$-excitation family and the $(\qafm,E_{12})$ emission peak---must
vanish there; the geometry-B same-polarised channel should be a single
harmonic peak; and adiabatically long pulses collapse the geometry-A
cross peak.  The temperature scan through the reorientation is the
decisive one, since it separates the symmetry-broken from the
conversion-generated features and measures $d_z^{\rm eff}$ by the size of
the collapse.

\section{Conclusion}

Two symmetry-selected Fe--Tm vertices, one Fe-sector magnon--magnon
coupling, a mirror-parity rule assigning each polarisation to one qutrit
block, and a hyperpolarisability allowed by the absence of inversion at
the rare-earth site account for the measured 2DCS atlas of TmFeO$_3$,
computed from measured inputs with no free lineshape parameters and one
residual dynamical ratio.  Every peak has been given a pathway, every
structural absence a selection rule, and every assignment a falsifiable
signature.  The program---bound the vertex space by symmetry, assign
every feature and every absence, and let the atlas overdetermine the
model---applies to any magnet whose 2D map spans distinct subsystems, and
turns 2DCS from a spectroscopy of modes into a spectroscopy of
Hamiltonian terms.
